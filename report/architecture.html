GramUrja AI · System Architecture

How it works

Three mechanisms: the hourly loop that dispatches energy, the offline search that decides what hardware to install, and the agent layer that will sit above both. The first two are built; the third is designed and not yet written.

The hourly dispatch loop

built

Every simulated hour the optimiser solves a 24-hour plan, applies only the first hour of it, and throws the rest away. Next hour it re-solves with a new battery state and a new forecast. The split that matters runs down the middle of this picture: the optimiser plans on what it believes, while the simulator runs on what actually happens.

The sizing search

built · runs once

Hardware is not chosen by hand. Every candidate combination of solar, wind and battery is run through an entire simulated year of the loop above, costed with capital recovered over each asset’s life, and the cheapest one that holds reliability wins. This runs offline, in parallel, and produces the system specification.

The agent layer

designed · not built

The agent layer wraps the engine; it does not replace any part of it. Its job is everything around the decision — noticing when reality has drifted from plan, deciding when a re-plan is worth running, and turning a vector of set-points into a sentence a farmer can act on. The dispatch decision itself stays in the solver.

Why draw the forbidden path at all. The obvious way to build an “agentic” energy system is to let the model choose the numbers. That fails quietly: a plausible-looking set-point can violate a battery limit or strand a critical load, and nothing in the model catches it. Constraining agents to orchestration is the design decision this project would most want to defend.

What each agent needs from the engine already exists: the run log carries every flow hour by hour, the KPI module reduces it, and the optimiser is a single call taking state and a forecast and returning set-points. That call is the tool boundary.

  • Monitoring compares logged actuals against the plan the optimiser issued, and decides whether a deviation is worth acting on — a passing cloud is not, a feeder that failed to arrive is.
  • Planning chooses when to re-solve and under what assumptions, and is where a farmer’s request — “I need four hours of pumping before Thursday” — becomes a constraint handed to the solver.
  • Explanation converts the chosen set-points into a recommendation with its reason attached, so the advice can be judged rather than merely obeyed.
